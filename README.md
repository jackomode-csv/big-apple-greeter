## The idea behind the new site

Your old website was seven pages of computer code. Changing a sentence meant
opening that code and hoping you did not break anything around it.

The new site is built out of **blocks**. A block is one piece of a page: a
heading with a list under it, a photo with a caption, a closing invitation with
a button. Each page is just a stack of blocks, one after another.

That matters because blocks can be edited on their own, moved around, added and
removed, without touching anything else on the page. You are handling pieces of
a page rather than a wall of code.

## How to change something

Go to your website address and add `/admin` to the end:

```
https://jackomode-csv.github.io/big-apple-greeter/admin/
```

Sign in when it asks. You will see the pages listed down the left. Click one,
and the blocks that make up it appear.

Two pages can be edited this way today: **About Us** and **Become a Greeter**.
The rest of the site is being moved across to the same system, a page at a
time. Until a page appears in that list, changes to it go through Jack.

### Changing words

Click into any box and type. It works like any form you have filled in online.
When you are happy, click **Publish**. The live website updates about a minute
later.

### Moving a section

Every block has a handle you can drag. Pick a block up, drop it where you want
it, and the page reorders. So if you decide the volunteer stories should come
before the office roles, you drag it above them. Nothing else moves.

### Adding something new

There is a **+** button under the list of blocks. Click it, choose the kind of
block you want, and fill it in. The choices are named in plain words, like
"Content section" or "Hero with background photo".

### Adding a row to a list

Lists, like the board of directors or the volunteer roles, have their own **+**
button. Click it and you get a fresh empty row to fill in.

### Removing something

Each block has a menu with a delete option. Nothing is ever really lost. Every
change is kept, so anything deleted can be brought back.

## What you can safely change

- Any wording anywhere: headings, paragraphs, list items, button labels
- Names, job titles, phone numbers, email addresses
- Board members and volunteer roles: add, remove, reorder
- Blog links and their dates
- The order of sections on a page
- The text that shows in a Google result

## What to leave alone, or ask Jack about first

- The videos, and the play and mute buttons on them
- The map on the homepage
- The two sign-up forms, for visitors and for volunteers
- Anything to do with how the site looks: colours, spacing, fonts

These are wired together carefully behind the scenes. They are not fragile, but
they are not the sort of thing to experiment with on a live website.

---

## A fair warning about the design

You cannot drag things anywhere you like, the way you might in Squarespace or
Canva. You can reorder blocks and change what is inside them, but you cannot
put a text box at any spot on the page.

This is deliberate. It is the reason the site will still look designed in two
years, after a hundred small edits by different people. The layout holds itself
together instead of relying on everyone being careful.

---

## If something goes wrong

Nothing you do in the editor can permanently break the site. Every version is
kept, so anything can be put back the way it was.

If the site looks wrong and you are not sure why, stop and contact Jack rather
than trying to fix it. Undoing one thing is quick. Undoing several attempted
repairs is not.
